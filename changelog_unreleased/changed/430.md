- SPEC §3.12 now keys the surviving-mutant obligation on what a wrong allow would cost rather than on whether a guard enforces a settled contract. A high-cost guard — one whose weakening lets a reachable value read, fetch or execute what its contract does not admit; create a link or an artifact outside its scope, leave an artifact behind past cleanup, or expose one to another user; hand a child an excluded environment, configuration or input; run a child without a bound or past its settled bound; mutate a target before its transition; or report a false success or cleanup confirmation — is owed a boundary test and a killed weakening. Any other guard owes no mutation-kill evidence and its survivors are recorded as decisions. Guards include preventive controls as well as predicates, a weakening is judged by what it newly admits or no longer stops rather than by its syntax, an added operation is not a survivor, and behavioural evidence, criterion-stated obligations, actual defects and the false-red and support-apparatus rules are unchanged.
