- SPEC §3.12 now keys the surviving-mutant obligation on what a wrong allow would cost rather than on whether a guard enforces a settled contract: a high-cost guard — one whose weakening lets a reachable value read, fetch or execute what its contract does not admit, create or expose a forbidden artifact, hand a child an excluded environment, run a child unbounded, mutate a target early, or report a false success — is owed a boundary test and a killed weakening, and any other guard owes no mutation-kill evidence, its survivors recorded as decisions. Guards include preventive controls as well as predicates, a weakening is judged by what it newly admits rather than by its syntax, an added operation is not a survivor, and behavioural evidence, criterion-stated obligations, actual defects and the false-red and support-apparatus rules are unchanged.
